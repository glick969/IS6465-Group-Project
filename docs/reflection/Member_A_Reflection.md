# Member A reflection contribution

The Product Management interface raises ethical questions about who can see business information, how mistakes are handled, and whether the interface gives an accurate impression of its capabilities. The staff design includes manufacturing costs and responsible employees because those details support internal work. The customer catalog shows active products, specifications, selling prices, and availability. It omits manufacturing costs and employee details. This is an interface boundary in the prototype; the final application must enforce the same boundary on the server, including direct requests to protected pages.

Critical thinking is especially important when removing a product. A simple delete button could hide the consequences for material requirements, order history, returns, and reporting. The removal screen identifies the product and explains its dependencies before asking for confirmation. A product with historical transactions can be marked inactive, and material requirements block permanent deletion. Keeping a visible Cancel action supports deliberate decisions. Current prices and historical order-line prices must also remain separate so later price changes do not rewrite earlier transactions.

Input design should help users recover from mistakes. Labels identify required fields, amounts are labeled in USD, and finished inventory is explicitly measured in whole pairs. Invalid submissions retain entered values and identify the fields needing correction. Text labels accompany status colors, and visible focus styles, keyboard-accessible controls, and responsive layouts support a wider range of users. These decisions require review with actual users rather than an assumption that a polished page is automatically accessible.

Only fictional data are used. The interface explains that saves are temporary and that the prototype has no database or real authentication. This avoids encouraging users to enter real credentials or treating a demonstration as a production inventory system. AI assistance was used to draft code, documents, diagrams, and verification steps.
